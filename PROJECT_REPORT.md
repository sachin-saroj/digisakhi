# DigiSakhi: Your Digital Safety Companion

## Problem statement

Smartphone access creates meaningful opportunities for communication, financial inclusion and public services, but many women in self-help groups also face unfamiliar scam messages, unsafe payment requests, privacy risks and uncertainty about where to seek support. Existing cyber-safety guidance is often technical, fragmented or difficult to access during a stressful incident. DigiSakhi addresses this communication and confidence gap through a simple, supportive digital-safety platform.

## Objective

The objective of DigiSakhi is to educate and empower women’s self-help-group members to use smartphones and online services more safely. The system presents short learning modules, knowledge checks and progress feedback, while also providing incident reporting, emergency contacts, a private suspicious-message checker and a peer-support forum. An administrator interface enables trainers to publish alerts and monitor programme-level engagement.

## Target beneficiaries

The primary beneficiaries are women who participate in self-help groups and who use smartphones for communication, digital payments, social media and access to public or financial services. Secondary beneficiaries include SHG coordinators, trainers and community facilitators who need a clear way to share timely safety information and identify areas where additional education may be helpful.

## Scope

The platform covers safe browsing, social-media privacy, scams and phishing, UPI and digital-payment safety, protection of personal photos and data, cyberbullying awareness and cybercrime reporting. Members can complete lessons, attempt quizzes, monitor progress, participate in a moderated community space and submit reports anonymously. The toolkit highlights women’s helpline 181, police helpline 1091 and cyber-fraud helpline 1930. A Quick Exit action supports discreet navigation away from the app. Administrators can manage learning content, review incident statuses, view aggregate analytics and broadcast urgent notices.

## Technology stack

DigiSakhi uses a React and Vite client with Tailwind CSS, accessible UI primitives and responsive mobile-first layout patterns. The application scaffold uses an Express server, tRPC contracts, TypeScript, Drizzle ORM and a managed SQL database. Authentication is provided through the project’s protected session flow. The interface uses a local rule-based heuristic for the scam checker so that pasted messages are not transmitted to a paid external AI service. English and Hindi are treated as first-class interface languages.

## Social impact

DigiSakhi promotes practical digital confidence rather than fear. Its core intervention is a repeatable habit: pause, verify and ask for help before acting on an unfamiliar request. Anonymous reporting reduces the social cost of disclosing a harmful incident, while the community forum makes peer learning visible and normal. Trainer-led alerts help translate emerging risks into timely local guidance. Together, these features support safer participation in digital payments, communication and online services, contributing to the broader goals of digital inclusion, financial safety and community resilience.

## Conclusion

DigiSakhi demonstrates how an approachable, bilingual and safety-conscious web application can support a community education programme. The project combines accessible interaction design with a clear technical architecture and a socially responsible approach to privacy, reporting and digital literacy.
